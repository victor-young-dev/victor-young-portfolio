About

I don't stop at
"that's a good idea".

That's the honest summary. Somebody says something interesting in a conversation and by the evening I've named it, sketched the homepage and worked out how it makes money. Then the real work starts.

I'm a designer, developer and video editor who kept refusing to pick one.

I studied Computer Science at Hezekiah University in Umudi and started taking freelance software jobs while I was still in school. For four years every single job came from someone recommending me. I have never run an advert.

What changed things was noticing what clients actually kept asking for. They didn't want a developer. They wanted somebody to work out what the thing should be called, what it stood for, what it should look like, how it should feel to use, how it made money — and then to build it. Those are five different jobs, and hiring five people to do them is how a good idea turns into an incoherent one.

So I learned all five. Not equally well — I'm a stronger builder than I am an illustrator, and I'd hire a better cinematographer than me any day. But well enough that nothing gets lost between the stages, which is where most projects actually die.

Today I run RayzorVerse, a small studio, and I build my own products under it. Kaabo and ResQNet are mine. Everything else is client work, and I'm careful about the difference.

How I'm wired

The useful parts, and the difficult ones.

I'd rather you know both before we work together.

01

I finish the thought

An idea isn't done when it's interesting. It's done when it has a name, a positioning, an interface, a business model and a URL. That instinct is the reason there are things on this site you can actually click.

02

My range is wide — sometimes too wide

Commerce, safety tech, EdTech, cybersecurity, branding, media, agriculture. Ideas are not my bottleneck; choosing between them is. I know that about myself, which is why I built a system to force the choice.

03

I'm difficult about how things look

Not "does it work" — does this block take up too much room, does this read as generated, does this feel real. I'll rebuild a section that functions perfectly because the spacing is lying about the hierarchy.

04

I work under constraint on purpose

Much of my work is written on a phone, with a laptop when one is free. That shaped everything: small phases, tight files, interfaces that hold up on cheap devices and bad networks. It's a discipline now, not a limitation.

What I believe

Four rules I don't bend.

Ship in phasesA phase that goes live beats a masterplan that doesn't. Every project gets broken into pieces small enough to finish in one sitting, and each piece gets deployed.
Security isn't phase twoPermissions, database rules and payment handling get built with the feature. I've closed privilege-escalation holes in my own work; I'd rather find them before a user does.
Design for the cheap phoneIf it only looks right on a MacBook, it doesn't work. Your customer is on a mid-range Android with two bars of signal.
Never fake the proofNo invented citations, no screenshots of things that don't exist, no "launching soon" on something that hasn't been started. Live means live.
The system behind it

I built an operating system for myself.

A Telegram bot that logs my day, splits income, enforces limits and tracks the client pipeline. It exists because I know exactly how I fail — too many ideas, scattered energy, and willpower I don't trust. So I don't rely on willpower. I build the guardrail.
