How I work

From idea to live.

Five stages. Most studios hand off between two or three different people here, and something gets lost at every handover. I carry it through.

Stage 01

Idea

What's actually broken, who feels it, and what they do about it today. Sometimes this stage kills the idea. That's a good outcome.

Stage 02

Name & brand

What it's called, what it stands for and how it sounds. Settled before a single screen gets designed, so the product and the pitch agree.

Stage 03

Interface

Screens, states, motion and type as one system. Designed for a cheap phone on a bad connection, because that's where the customer is.

Stage 04

Build

Real software, shipped in phases. Permissions and security rules are part of the build, not a promise for later.

Stage 05

Live

On its own domain, handed over with the code, then watched and fixed while real people use it for the first time.

Every project I've ever done came from someone recommending me. I've never run an advert, and I'd like to keep it that way.

I started as a Computer Science student taking freelance software jobs. It turned into something bigger when clients kept asking for the parts nobody else would do — the name, the positioning, the interface, the copy — and there was no one to hand the other halves to.

A good part of what's on this site was written on a phone, with a laptop when one was free. That's not a hardship story, it's a design constraint. It's why my interfaces work on cheap devices and slow connections, and why I build in phases that can each be finished in one sitting.
